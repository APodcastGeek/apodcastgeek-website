The Situation

The Reality

Lines keep stopping and nobody on the floor can give a clear answer as to why. The data exists somewhere in the system, but translating it into a root cause takes time that most operations teams do not have. The result is a cycle of reactive fixes that solve the symptom and leave the actual cause in place.

Operations directors at manufacturers with 50 to 500 staff carry the pressure of output targets while managing teams that are stretched thin across multiple shifts. When a fault recurs on the main line, the cost is not just the downtime itself. It is the overtime, the customer conversations, and the knock-on effect across every downstream process.

The standard response is to add monitoring, run more shift reviews, or bring in a generalist consultant who maps everything and recommends everything. None of that addresses the two or three specific points on the line where the real damage is happening. The problem stays unsolved because the focus stays too wide.

The Approach

How It Works

The Constraint Removal System
1

Map the Line

We start by building a clear picture of where time is actually being lost across the full production line. This is not a high-level audit. It targets the specific handoff points, machine cycles, and shift patterns that generate the unplanned stops your team is dealing with week to week.

2

Find the Two or Three Constraints

The downtime is not caused by the whole line. It is caused by two or three hidden constraint points that trigger a cascade every time they fail. We identify those specific points using the line map and production data, and we prioritise them by impact so the team knows exactly where to act first.

3

Remove Them in Sequence

Constraints are removed one at a time in the order that delivers the greatest reduction in downtime. Each removal is verified before moving to the next, so the team can see the improvement in real numbers before the 90 day window closes. The result is a line that runs to plan rather than reacting to faults that repeat themselves.

Case Study
Halton Engineering
Before

Halton Engineering was losing 14 hours a week to unplanned stops on the main line. The faults were not consistent in type, which made it difficult for the team to identify a pattern or target a single cause. Shift handovers were being consumed by troubleshooting rather than planned production activity.

Results

After applying the Constraint Removal System, downtime fell by 34 percent in six weeks. The team recovered 9 hours of productive line time per week, and the recurring faults that had been driving the unplanned stops were removed at the source rather than patched.

Case Study
Ardmore Plastics
Before

Ardmore Plastics had three shifts firefighting the same recurring fault across the production line. The fault had been present long enough that the team treated it as a fixed cost of running the line. Significant time and resource were going into managing the symptom on each shift rather than eliminating the underlying cause.

Results

The root cause of the fault was identified and removed within the engagement period. Downtime dropped by 28 percent in two months, and the shift teams were able to focus on planned activity rather than reacting to a fault they had stopped expecting to solve.
