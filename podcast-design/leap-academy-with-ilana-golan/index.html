You will learn

To think bigger about what is possible, learn from people who have taken bold leaps, and walk away with actionable insights to accelerate your career and build success on your own terms.

The design

01

The leak is awareness, through your packaging

Your guests are strong. What our research found is that people are not finding the episodes, and the packaging (the thumbnail and the title) is where that happens.

Example
Benchmark Partner, Bill Gurley: The Strategy That Makes You Stand Out in a Crowded Job Market
Ilana Golan, Leap Academy · 12,600 subscribers
288views
I'm 55 Years Old and I Can't Find a Job
Man Over Fifty · 2,510 subscribers
93,963views

Views as of 29 September 2026. Click either thumbnail to open it on YouTube.

Both are full-length videos about the job market. The winner is a talking-head video, and the point still stands: packaging wins when it deliberately opens a curiosity gap. That is what we are aiming for, to grow awareness of your show in the market.

So we package every episode from its North Star, the one big thing the conversation delivers, and find the angle in it that creates curiosity.

Where the demand is right now

Midlife career change

About 20,000 YouTube searches a month, and vidIQ marks it as growing. The videos ranking for it sit between 5,000 and 13,000 views, so the demand is there and nobody owns it yet.

After a layoff

"Laid off" gets about 44,700 YouTube searches a month. Mel Robbins' episode on what to do next has 360,754 views.

Portfolio careers

About 4,800 searches a month. Small, but every video ranking for it is on topic, and it is exactly what Leap Academy teaches.

Fractional roles

"How to become a fractional executive" gets about 5,200 searches a month with very little competition. No video on it has passed 10,000 views yet.

Search numbers are vidIQ's estimates of monthly YouTube searches. View counts are from YouTube. Both pulled 28 September 2026.

How the content earns its way

Every episode moves people down the same funnel.

Top of funnel

Entertain

The guest intro and the story behind it. This earns the click and holds the first minute.

Mid funnel

Educate

The lessons inside the story: the catalyst, the why and the mindset. Viewers take these away to move their own careers forward.

Bottom of funnel

Sell

For the people who stay. They believe in the Leap Academy message and want the same for themselves, but need help. That is where your offer comes in.

03

Interview lanes

Where you are now

The current lane

Your conversations follow each guest's life in order: childhood, the journey, the hard moments, then leadership and scale.

Of your last 30 guests, 18 are founders who built their own companies and 6 were hired into the top job.

Where we would take it

The proposed lane

Each episode is built around one leap your audience is searching for, such as a midlife career change, life after a layoff, a fractional role or a portfolio career.

The conversation shows what made the guest take that leap, why they took it and how they did it, so your viewers can do the same.

How each episode gets its direction

Before every recording, you run the guest through our episode brief generator. It reads this guide, researches the guest and gives you five directions for the episode, at least two of them built on the demand in Part B section 1. You pick one, and the brief it produces sets the questions.

Guest mix

Keep the founder stories that bring in your reach, and add one guest a month who has made the move your audience wants: from senior manager to the C-suite, onto a board, or into a portfolio career. These are the stories your viewers can copy, and the people most likely to want your training.

03Sign-off

The Leap Question

Each guest answers a question left by the guest before them, then leaves one for the next. It is always about making the leap: a mindset question, a catalyst question (what finally made you move) or a why question.

Borrowed from The Diary of a CEO
Prompt for generating a Pivot Point question

Paste this into ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini, then add your guest's details and the listener questions you have received.

You are helping me prepare the Pivot Point segment for my podcast, Leap Academy with Ilana Golan. In Pivot Point, a listener's career question is put to my guest, who answers it first, and then I add my own take.

I will give you:
1. My guest's name, role, and LinkedIn or website
2. The listener questions I have received (emails, comments or DMs), pasted in full
3. Optional: the episode brief for this guest

My audience: professionals, mostly 35 to 44, who know they are capable of more and want their next move to be bigger, whether that is a C-suite role, a board seat, an advisory role, a fractional role or a portfolio career.

Your job:
1. Read the listener questions and pick the three this guest is best placed to answer from their own career. Give one line on why for each.
2. Tidy each question so it can be read aloud in under 20 seconds. Keep the listener's meaning and first name. Do not add details they did not give.
3. For each question, suggest one follow-up I can ask the guest, and one angle I could add from my own experience.

Rules:
- Only use real listener questions I paste in. Never invent a listener or a question.
- If I have not pasted any, tell me, and suggest three questions I could ask my audience on social media this week to collect them.
- Every question must be about making a career leap: getting unstuck, changing direction, getting hired or promoted, going fractional or portfolio, or the mindset behind it.
- Plain language, no jargon.

Guest intros

Sixty seconds, narration over clips, at the top of every episode. It does four things in order.

  1. 1
    Confirms what the title and thumbnail promised, so the viewer knows straight away they are in the right place.
  2. 2
    Establishes who the guest is and why they are worth an hour.
  3. 3
    Says what gets discussed and why it matters, without turning it into a lecture.
  4. 4
    Gets into the conversation fast.

Because the intro handles the credibility, you never read a bio on mic. After it plays, you go straight into your first question.

Elements inside it: text animations, generated footage where it helps tell the story, and motion graphics that animate what is being said.
