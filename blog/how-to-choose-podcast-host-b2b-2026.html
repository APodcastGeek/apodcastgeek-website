A podcast host is the service that stores your finished episodes and sends them to Apple Podcasts, Spotify and the other directories through an RSS feed. It is not your recording tool, your editor or your guest booking system.

For a B2B show, that distinction matters. Founders often shop for "podcast software" and buy the wrong thing first. Hosting is the distribution pipe. Strategy, guests, video and clips sit upstream of it.

What a podcast host does

A podcast host typically:

  • stores your audio (and sometimes video) episode files
  • publishes an RSS feed directories can read
  • submits or guides submission to Apple Podcasts, Spotify and similar apps
  • shows download or listener stats
  • gives you a public show page and episode pages

It usually does not:

  • recruit guests in your Ideal Customer Profile
  • brief hosts and guests before recording
  • produce video, audio masters or short-form clips
  • write show notes or SEO articles
  • run ads or pipeline follow-up

If you only need a place to publish finished episodes, a host is enough. If you need the full production system, hosting is one line item inside a bigger stack.

What B2B founders should check before they pick a host

Ignore feature lists with forty toggles. Check these four points.

Reliability and RSS

Your RSS feed is how directories stay in sync. A broken feed means Apple and Spotify stop updating. Look for a host with a clean track record, clear documentation and support that answers when something fails.

Analytics you will actually read

B2B teams need stats they can explain in a monthly review. Downloads per episode, listener geography and device mix are the basics. Fancy charts that nobody opens are worthless.

Team access and permissions

If more than one person touches the show, you need seats and roles. A solo founder login shared in Slack becomes a problem the first time someone leaves.

Fit with a video-first workflow

The B2B shows we produce are usually video first, then audio. Your host should accept clean audio masters from your edit, and your YouTube workflow should sit beside that, not fight it. Hosting audio on one platform and ignoring YouTube is how shows stay small.

Buzzsprout for DIY B2B shows

If your team will record and edit in-house, and you only need reliable hosting plus directory distribution, Buzzsprout is a solid default.

Teams use Buzzsprout to upload finished episodes, keep RSS stable and get the show onto Apple Podcasts, Spotify and the other major apps. The interface is clear. Support is practical. Pricing is predictable for a founder-led show.

Use Buzzsprout when:

  • you already have a repeatable way to record and edit
  • someone on your team owns publishing every week or fortnight
  • you want hosting without building custom infrastructure

Skip Buzzsprout (or any host-only plan) as your whole strategy when:

  • nobody has time to recruit and brief guests
  • you need five short-form clips, thumbnails and an article per episode
  • you want guest relationships treated as pipeline, not "content"
